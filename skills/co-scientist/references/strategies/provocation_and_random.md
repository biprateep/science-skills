# Provocation, Random Input, & Reverse Assumptions

These techniques are "pattern breakers." Use them when mathematical derivations hit a dead end, or when assumptions in a physical model limit progress.

## 1. Reverse Assumptions
List fundamental axioms or assumptions, then deliberately reverse each one to see what new physics or math emerges.

**Examples in Physics/Math:**
- *Assumption*: Time flows in one direction (T-symmetry is broken macroscopically).
  - *Reverse*: What if we assume T-symmetry holds and run the simulation backward to infer initial states from final observations?
- *Assumption*: Space is continuous.
  - *Reverse*: What if space is fundamentally discrete (Loop Quantum Gravity, lattice gauge theory)?
- *Assumption*: The loss landscape is continuous and differentiable.
  - *Reverse*: What if it's entirely discrete, forcing us to use Boolean satisfiability or evolutionary algorithms instead of gradient descent?

## 2. Provocation Technique
Use deliberately absurd or impossible statements to break mental patterns.

**Format**: "Po (Provocation Operation) + [impossible statement]"

**Examples:**
- Po: The simulation runs instantly with infinite precision. (What approximation gets us closest to this? Neural surrogate models?)
- Po: The data labels itself. (Self-supervised learning).
- Po: Gravity repels instead of attracts. (Dark energy cosmological models).

**Then ask:** "What's mathematically interesting about this?" and "How could we formulate a limit that approaches this?"

## 3. Future Backwards
Imagine it's 10 years in the future and the theorem has been proved (or the unified theory formulated). Work backwards.

**Questions:**
- What lemma had to be proved first?
- What physical anomaly finally gave the critical clue?
- What unexpected mathematical bridge (e.g., between topology and algebra) made it possible?
- Assume the solution exists: what properties *must* it possess? (Existence and uniqueness proofs).
