# Six Thinking Hats

A method for exploring ideas from six distinct perspectives, ensuring comprehensive analysis. Have the scientist metaphorically "wear" different hats to shift thinking modes.

### White Hat (Facts and Information)
- What data do we have?
- What are the known axioms or theorems?
- What measurements or statistical baselines exist?
- **Usage:** Establish the rigorous mathematical or empirical baseline.

### Red Hat (Emotions and Intuition)
- What is the mathematical intuition telling us?
- Does this proof strategy *feel* elegant?
- What seems promising based on gut feeling, even before writing the code?
- **Usage:** Allow intuitive leaps without immediate formal justification.

### Black Hat (Critical Judgment)
- What are the edge cases where this equation blows up?
- Why might this algorithm fail to converge?
- What are the statistical confounders we are ignoring?
- **Usage:** Identify potential flaws, singularities, and failure modes.

### Yellow Hat (Optimistic View)
- If this model scales perfectly, what is the theoretical limit?
- What are the computational benefits of this approach?
- Why might this symmetry assumption lead to a breakthrough?
- **Usage:** Explore the theoretical maximum bounds and positive outcomes.

### Green Hat (Creativity)
- What alternatives exist?
- What wild topological shapes could we map this to?
- What if we break conservation of energy in the simulation just to see what happens?
- **Usage:** Generate novel, unconstrained mathematical frameworks.

### Blue Hat (Process Control)
- What's the big picture of this derivation?
- What intermediate lemma do we need to prove next?
- How do we structure the computational pipeline?
- **Usage:** Step back to synthesize, structure proofs, and plan experiments.
