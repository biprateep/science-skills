# Counterexamples — sentences the author rejected

Sentences co-writer produced that the author rejected, each with his objection
verbatim. The digest ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), "Maintenance") moves them here
from the session log's `Note` field and from `corrections.md`; nothing is
paraphrased.

**How this file is read.** Once, after a draft is written, as examples of what
missed — never as a list to revise against. A passage revised against a list
gets worse. Where a sentence of yours does what one of these does, re-read the
specimen for that slot in [voice-profile.md](voice-profile.md) and write the
stretch again.

**Entry form.** The sentence as delivered; the slot; the objection, verbatim;
what he accepted instead, if he wrote one. Entries with the same fault are
merged, the objections kept side by side.

## Introduction opener

### 1. Author-by-author roll call vs. task-first parenthetical grouping
- **Slot:** intro-opener
- **Sentence as delivered:**
  > For example, \citet{Benitez2000} combined photometric likelihoods with population priors to estimate galaxy redshifts from imaging, \citet{ForemanMackey2014} inferred exoplanet occurrence rates from noisy and incomplete transit catalogs, and \citet{Hogg2010} formulated linear regression as a probabilistic model of how latent variables, measurement uncertainties, and outliers generate observed data.
- **Objection (author, verbatim):**
  > If you look at my older papers, I do not generally cite things one by one like you have done. For example, [Person A] combined this with that to get [Thing], [Person B] did this and that. Whereas my general philosophy is, for example, write the application case and then in (e.g., ...) put a bunch of relevant citations or one citation, so on and so forth. So let's follow that idea.
  > The only thing I would say in the sentence when we are saying common examples include, we should make it clear that this is not an exhaustive list, and maybe we might be able to add some more general use cases and add more than one citation in the background... arrange that sentence around some common tasks and do a quick literature survey and verify the citations to include more than one example of each task, and we always use e.g. and then cite two or three papers depending on the situation.
- **Accepted instead:**
  > Representative examples include predicting physical properties such as redshifts, stellar masses, or chemical abundances of individual objects from observed photometry or spectra \citep[e.g.,][]{Benitez2000, GarciaPerez2016, Leja2017}, inferring intrinsic population distributions and occurrence rates from noisy and incomplete survey catalogs across exoplanets, Galactic stars, or gravitational-wave sources \citep[e.g.,][]{ForemanMackey2014, Bovy2016, Thrane2019, Rix2021}, and fitting scaling relations in the presence of measurement uncertainties, intrinsic scatter, and outliers \citep[e.g.,][]{Akritas1996, Kelly2007, Hogg2010}, among many others.
